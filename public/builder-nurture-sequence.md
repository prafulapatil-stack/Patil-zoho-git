## Email 2 — The Dal: Why Builders Skip the Most Important Dish

* **Target Audience:** Builder Phase (Aged 30–45)
* **Goal:** Emphasize emergency funds, term coverage, and medical protection
* **Timing:** Day 3
* **Subject:** `The dish most Builders skip — and why it costs them everything`

### Body Copy
Hello ${contact.firstName},

Last time, we spoke about your Builder Thali. Today, let's talk about the dish that almost every ambitious professional ignores — the Dal.

In a Thali, Dal is not glamorous. It is not the Thecha that gets attention or the Sweet Dish that gets celebrated. But remove it from the plate and the whole meal collapses.

In your wealth portfolio, Dal is your emergency fund and term insurance. It is the non-negotiable foundation that every other investment rests on.

Here is what we see regularly: A 35-year-old professional with ₹50,000 per month in SIPs — excellent — but no term cover and three months of EMIs stacked up with zero liquid buffer. One job transition, one medical event, one market correction at the wrong time, and the entire SIP portfolio gets redeemed at a loss to cover expenses.

Your Dal must be in place before you add more spice.

### The Builder's Dal Checklist:
1. **Emergency Fund:** 6 months of total expenses in a liquid or arbitrage fund
2. **Term Insurance:** Minimum 10× annual income, pure term, no ULIPs
3. **Health Insurance:** Independent of employer cover — because jobs change

## Email 3 — The Thecha: How Much Equity Spice Is Right for You?

* **Target Audience:** Builder Phase (Aged 30–45)
* **Goal:** Frame high-growth equity, asset allocation, and discipline through SIPs
* **Timing:** Day 7
* **Subject:** `The Thecha question every Builder gets wrong`

### Body Copy
Hello ${contact.firstName},

If the Dal is the foundation of your Thali, the Thecha is its engine — high-growth equity that gives your wealth the fire it needs to compound aggressively over the next 15–20 years.

But here is the question most Builders get wrong: how much Thecha is the right amount?

Too little and your portfolio grows slower than inflation — you are eating a bland meal that will not sustain you in retirement. Too much and a market correction in year 3 of your SIP journey triggers panic redemptions that set you back five years.

### The Builder's Equity Framework:
* **Age 30–35:** Up to 75–80% equity allocation is appropriate
* **Age 36–40:** 65–75% equity, beginning to layer in quality large-cap
* **Age 41–45:** 55–65% equity, increasing hybrid and balanced fund allocation
* **Core/Satellite:** 60–70% core (large-cap, flexi-cap index) + 20–30% satellite (mid-cap, small-cap)

The Thecha works best when it is consistent — a SIP that runs through corrections without stopping is worth ten times the lumpsum invested at the "right" time.
